About

I’m Cheng Chen (Martin), a researcher at the Centre for Frontier AI Research (CFAR), A*STAR. I study how to make foundation models more reliable and useful when labeled data and human feedback are scarce.

My work asks how related examples can help a model interpret an ambiguous input, how language models can adapt to individual preferences, and how we can check their annotations when reference labels are unavailable.

Outside research, I enjoy basketball and football, following economics and finance, and ferreting out good food.

Research directions

  • Personalization and reliable predictions. How can a language model learn what one person prefers from just a few examples? I also study whether agreement between an LLM and a task-specific encoder can flag annotations that need a second look when reference labels are unavailable.
  • Learning and recalibration from weak LLM supervision. When can a model learn from its own imperfect predictions? Self-Teaching Prompting looks for predictions that agree across repeated runs. BIDS-ICL asks when a coherent batch gives the model enough context and when a suggested label helps.
  • Understanding what models learn. RRInf asks which training examples influence a language model’s output or a generated image. MarineLife-16K tests whether video-language models can tell an animal’s movement from the movement of the camera.
